\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A general-government balance is normally reported as a single number, although it is
the sum of institutionally distinct subsectors whose balances are determined by
different revenue bases, different expenditure mandates and different legal
constraints. This paper asks how Portugal's general-government balance has been
composed across those subsectors over \PanelYears\ years, and how persistent each
subsector's contribution has been.

\noindent
We assemble a harmonised annual panel of net lending and net borrowing for General
Government and its three subsectors from \PanelStart\ to \PanelEnd, keeping the
\textbf{1995} statistical splice explicit rather than chaining across it, and
decompose the aggregate balance exactly into its subsector components and their
annual changes. The decomposition is an accounting identity, not a model: it
reallocates a recorded quantity and estimates nothing.

\noindent
Four results stand out. First, the composition is highly asymmetric and stable:
the aggregate balance is positive in only \NumPositiveYears\ of \PanelYears\ years
(\PositiveYearList), the Central Government balance is negative in all
\CentralNegativeYears, and the Social Security balance is positive in
\SsfPositiveYears. Second, in every year with a positive aggregate balance the
combined non-Social-Security balance is negative and the Social Security balance
exceeds it in absolute size; we quantify this as an offset ratio and decline to
read it as a counterfactual. Third, magnitudes differ sharply across the 1995
splice --- the aggregate balance averages \GgMeanHistorical\% of GDP before it and
\GgMeanModern\% after --- so no level statistic is pooled across the boundary.
Fourth, the permanently negative Central Government headline balance does not imply
a permanently negative underlying one: once interest is excluded, the Central
Government primary balance is positive in \PrimaryPositiveYears\ of the
\PrimaryObservedYears\ years for which detailed accounts exist, spread across the
panel rather than confined to recent years.

\noindent
We then separate two Social Security objects that are routinely conflated, the
ESA 2010 Social Security Funds sector and the budget-execution systems published
by the Portuguese Public Finance Council, and show that they differ in every year
they overlap. No causal, normative or policy-intent interpretation is assigned to
any result. Every figure and table is generated from a persisted artefact, and
every number in this text is a macro read from one.
